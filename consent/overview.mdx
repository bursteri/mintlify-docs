Use Plainrouter's consent management platform (CMP) to give visitors a choice, apply their decisions to supported browser tags, and inspect the choices your banner records. Bring together banner controls, saved preferences, consent receipts and cookie declarations in one workspace.

You can use the included Plainrouter banner or connect a supported external CMP. Setup uses the workspace's generated browser snippet; the banner controls are currently under **Signals → Install → Consent banner**. A Meta ad account connection is needed only for workflows that use Meta, such as advertising delivery.

## What does the CMP include?

| Capability | What you can configure or inspect |
| --- | --- |
| Visitor choices | **Accept all**, **Reject all** and **Customize**, with separate Analytics and Marketing choices. Analytics alone does not grant advertising permissions. |
| Saved preferences and reopening | Restore the saved Plainrouter choice and offer a floating Privacy settings control or your own preferences button when the surface is enabled. A configured HTTPS privacy-policy link appears in the banner and preferences. |
| Banner coverage | The workspace owner can choose location-based coverage, **Always show**, or **Off — I handle consent myself**. Off disables automatic display, floating controls and manual opening while keeping stored decisions. |
| Browser-tag coordination | Use the generated Google Consent Mode and Microsoft UET instructions to apply defaults, saved choices and later updates. Other supported vendor updates depend on their browser APIs being present. |
| Consent reporting | Inspect recorded Analytics and Marketing choices, location/language/policy breakdowns, recent receipts and CSV exports. The reporting window is 180 days on every plan. |
| Cookie details in Customize | The English preferences view can show host-scoped cookie and storage entries grouped by category and provider. It loads on opening Customize and keeps consent choices usable if the optional list fails. |
| Cookie inventory and declarations | Scan website cookie and storage metadata, inspect before-consent and after-accept coverage, and publish the declaration with per-host freshness. Cookie scans and the public declaration are available on every plan. |

The included banner's Marketing choice maps to `ad_storage`, `ad_user_data` and `ad_personalization`; Analytics maps to `analytics_storage`. On the consent-based advertising path, all three advertising permissions must be granted. Missing or unknown choices stay unknown. Follow the [consent-state reference](/signals/track-events#set-consent) when connecting your own interface.

## How do I get started?

1. Create the intended workspace and complete the [shared browser installation](/signals/install-pixel). Use its generated key, collection domain and complete snippet once.
2. Open **Signals → Install → Consent banner** to enable the included card and choose whether to show the floating reopen control. The workspace owner manages [banner coverage](/signals/install-pixel#how-do-i-choose-where-the-banner-appears) separately.
3. Copy the complete current installation and consent instructions. Follow the supplied [Google and UET tag controls](/signals/install-pixel#what-changes-for-google-and-microsoft-uet-tags); the banner cannot configure unrelated CMS plugins or tag-manager triggers for you.
4. Test acceptance, rejection, partial choices, returning saved choices and withdrawal with the [browser verification guide](/signals/track-events#verify-grant-partial-consent-and-withdrawal).
5. Read the recorded [consent choices](/signals/consent-choices), then [scan the website and publish its declaration](/signals/cookie-scan).

For a same-origin installation, the enabled banner appears for all visitors because that serving path cannot pass visitor location. **Off** still disables the Plainrouter surface. Verify the installed page after changing coverage; saving a workspace setting cannot rewrite an older pasted snippet.

## Can visitors inspect cookies before choosing?

In the current English banner, **Customize** can show cookie and storage names, providers and available retention details for the website host. Analytics and Marketing keep their separate choices; Functional and Unclassified groups are informational. The optional list does not load on the initial banner, on notice-only surfaces, when the banner is Off, or in other banner languages.

The list combines scan evidence with conditional documented Plainrouter entries. Unknown scans do not mean zero cookies, and the list does not automatically block other scripts or establish compliance. [Read freshness, loading and scan limits](/signals/cookie-scan#what-do-visitors-see-in-customize).

## Can I keep my existing CMP?

Yes, when you connect its decisions through a supported signal or explicit adapter. The snippet checks explicit consent, TCF and Consent Mode inputs before using its fallback card. A detected supported CMP without a usable decision suppresses that fallback and leaves advertising consent unresolved. A later valid external decision dismisses the Plainrouter card.

Use the [external-CMP integration guide](/signals/track-events#how-does-the-snippet-choose-a-consent-source). If you turn the included banner off, follow the [Off migration steps](/signals/install-pixel#what-must-i-change-when-turning-the-banner-off) so your CMP owns defaults, saved choices, updates and withdrawal. External CMP records remain separate from receipts generated by the Plainrouter card.

## How do I check the result?

Verify the visitor interface and the resulting requests separately. A visible card establishes that the interface loaded; inspect the recorded choice and subsequent browser behavior to check that the decision reached the integration. Withdrawal stops consent-dependent work, while requests already received by a destination cannot be recalled.

Use [Consent choices](/signals/consent-choices) for recorded decisions and exportable receipt metadata. Use [Cookie scan and declaration](/signals/cookie-scan) for observations about the pages the scanner visited. Read each host's freshness and phase coverage; incomplete or unknown scans keep their limits visible.
